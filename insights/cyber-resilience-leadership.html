Cyber resilience is a leadership decision

Cyber resilience improves when leaders connect technical exposure with business services, response decisions and accountability.

QUN Consulting | Editorial perspective | October 2026

Cyber resilience improves when leaders connect technical exposure with business services, response decisions and accountability.

Start with the business service

An inventory of technology is necessary, but it does not tell leadership which services are most important during disruption. Map critical services, dependencies, recovery expectations and decision owners first.

Connect prevention and response

A mature approach looks across identity, cloud, suppliers, detection, response and recovery. A weakness in one area can invalidate assumptions elsewhere.

Define practical measures

Track a small number of indicators such as critical asset coverage, high-risk remediation age, exercise outcomes, recoverability evidence and decision-making timeliness.

Questions to ask

Which services would create the greatest operational harm if unavailable? Who can authorize emergency actions? Have recovery priorities been tested, rather than only documented?

Putting the perspective into practice

Start with a well-defined scope, select a small number of high-value controls or decisions, assign accountable owners and review the outcome. Actual requirements vary by organization and applicable rules.
